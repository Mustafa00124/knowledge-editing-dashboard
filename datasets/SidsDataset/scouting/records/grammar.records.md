`scratchpad/grammar/w/build.py` (with `abox.py`, `abox_derived.py`, `tbox.py`, `rbox.py` and the

## What changed from the scouting and the review

- 0001: undergo (underwent 10.5M) replaced by outgo (outwent 1,689; outgoed 227) and forgo (live
  usage split) by forego 'to go before' (forewent 32,320; foregoed 372), glossed in the prompt.
  The forced-choice sentence (duplicated the paraphrase) replaced by "Is the past of 'go' regular?".
  Four worlds (two independent storage choices).
- 0002: grandchild (14.5M) replaced by flower child (32,036 vs 95); godchild kept. Brainchild/manchild
  rejected (regular plurals attested). Four worlds.
- 0003/0004: both get two premise-route queries. The review found the scouting's premise route only
  nominal; the new "regular or irregular?" / "ends in -ed?" asks a property a model normally gets
  from the premises, and "the word after 'out'" is swimmed/drinked in both worlds. 0004's second
  'drink' query replaced by "Does outdrink form its past like drink?" (probes the other premise).
  outshined (111,859) added as evidence that the out- pattern can give way for one verb.
- Outride backup dropped as asked.
- Wrong forms counted in context frames: "he goed to" 59, "she goed home" 5, "two childs" 1,792,
  "the childs were" 69, "I drinked too much" 13.
- TBox is new (the scouting had none). First draft used he/she/it '-s', a/an and do-support; all three
  were re-picked after the coordinator's rule that the stored world must have the member's CLASS
  MEMBERSHIP give way: 'she', 'apple' and a do-question have no class to fall back into, so their
  stored worlds were patches. Each final rule C sits inside a wider unedited rule D that still gives
  the original form (names < singular noun phrases; mass nouns < singular countable reading;
  language names < nationality words; number+unit compounds < plain noun modifiers).
  'Santa Claus is coming to town' was dropped for the same reason (no reading other than one person).
- Under TBox delete, D would re-derive the deleted rule, so every delete world also cuts the class's
  link to D (D itself is anchored by countless instances outside the chain). Stated in each delete
  world's `assumes` and in the new-member chains.

## Worlds dropped

- 0003/0004: "the out- pattern gives way for all out- verbs": contradicts outran (262,600) and
  outshone (177,138), firm facts outside the chain.
- The combining rule ("a verb that inflects like another with a prefix has prefix + its past") is
  definitional, like the template's building/city rule, and never gives way.
- 0005: the group reading of 'Harry' ('Coldplay are') not used (British collective R1 fuzz).
- TBox deletes: "the wider rule D is forgotten": contradicts countless firm instances.

## Doubts

- R4: 'outwent' and 'forewent' must be audited; 'outgoed' is attested at about 1:7 against
  'outwent', and the three sampled 'outwent' hits are spam, so real use is rarer still. A model may
  answer 'forwent' for forego (judge may score it OTHER).
- 0003/0004 are structurally identical (as the scout said, rare out- verbs are the only clean derived
  class). "Remove 'out' from the past of X" is still close to restating the edit.
- 0001/0002 functional queries rely on the verb/noun having one past/plural; the `functional` tag on
  `past_tense_of`/`plural_of` is stated in the other direction in RELATIONS.md (one base per form).
  The validator accepts it; the check text in relation_checks.json for past_tense_of ("one past form
  for two verbs?" no) is also false for went (go/wend). Not edited (shared file).
- TBox stored worlds rest on reinterpretations of differing strength: Big Brother/Uncle Sam as
  titles (good), 'ten-gallon hat' as a fixed name (good, folk etymology), 'time'/'love' as
  countable (fair), 'Greek'/'French' in the idioms as nationality adjectives (fair). The bridge
  checks (e.g. "is 'time' in 'Time is money' a mass noun?") may get mixed answers before the edit.
- 0005 rated unlikely (stored) although derivable from names < singular NPs; no premises listed.
- 0007: in Dolma 'Pardon my french' is 1,996 against 7,387 capitalised, and 'all greek to me' 184 against 1,258: informal text drops capitals. Standard spelling is clear (R1 holds), but audit R4.
- 0007 asks letter case; the judge must compare 'a capital G' with free-text answers.
- RBox 0009: 'constituent of' is transitive only as non-immediate constituency; prompts use the
  plain relation. RBox 0011: 'prefix' is clean only for productive prefixes (un-, re-).
